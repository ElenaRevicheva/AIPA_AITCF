## Proof sheet: medical tourism in Latin America

**1. How many people travel for care**
- **Millions** of US residents go abroad for medical care every year. The most common destinations are Mexico, the Caribbean and South America, plus Canada. *CDC Yellow Book, 2026 edition.* [link](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/health-care-abroad/medical-tourism.html)
- In a survey of 11 US states and territories, **1.32%** of adults (about **1,130,000 people**) had travelled abroad for planned care in one year. Of their trips, Mexico took **41%** (291 of 705) and Colombia **6%**. *CDC authors, Infection Control & Hospital Epidemiology, 2022, using 2016 data.* [link](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/115458/cdc_115458_DS1.pdf)

**2. Latin America leads**
- 4 of the top 5 countries where Americans spend money on health travel are in Latin America or the Caribbean: Mexico **36.5%**, Colombia **6.1%**, Dominican Republic **4.6%**, Costa Rica **4.4%**. *US Commerce Dept (NTTO/BEA), 2022 data.* [link](https://www.trade.gov/sites/default/files/2023-10/Travel-Trade-by-Category.pdf)
- Baja California (Mexico) counted **5,540,034** border visits for medical care in 2025, up from **3,378,092** in 2023. These are visits, not individual people, and they include doctor and pharmacy trips. *Observatorio Turístico de Baja California, INEGI data.* [link](https://observaturbc.com/Indicadores/afluencia_estacionalidad/visitantes/OTBC-AEVI-04%20Visitantes%20internacinales%20por%20motivo%20de%20viaje%20BC.xlsx)
- **26,786** foreigners entered Colombia for medical treatment in 2025, up **18.4%**. In January to August 2026 there were **21,034**, up **17.6%**. *Migración Colombia figures, published by ANATO, 28 Sep 2026.* [link](https://anato.org/noticias/colombia-se-posiciona-como-potencial-destino-de-turismo-en-salud-21-034-extranjeros-no-residentes-llegaron-al-pais-por-tratamientos-medicos-entre-enero-y-agosto-de-2026/)

**3. Dental**
- The CDC says dental care is the most common type of medical tourism among US residents. *CDC Yellow Book 2026* (same link as above).
- **42%** of Costa Rica's medical tourists come for dental treatment. *Cámara Costarricense de la Salud (an industry chamber), reported by La República, Feb 2025.* [link](https://www.larepublica.net/noticia/costa-rica-refuerza-su-liderazgo-con-foro-nacional-de-turismo-medico-2025)

**4. Cosmetic surgery**
- Plastic surgeons say foreign patients make up **33.3%** of their patients in Mexico and **31.5%** in Colombia. The world average is **15.3%**, so both are about double. Mexico is in the top 3 worldwide. *ISAPS Global Survey 2025, published 29 Sep 2026. These are surgeons' estimates, not arrival counts.* [link](https://www.isaps.org/media/ua4leneo/isaps-global-survey_2025.pdf)

**5. Panama**
- Panama's own tourism authority says "Panamá no es percibido como un destino de turismo médico." It rates Panama's image in source markets as weak and calls the sector early-stage. That rating is expert opinion, not a measurement. Panama's surgeons report **20.3%** foreign patients, mostly from the US and Colombia (ISAPS 2025). *ATP Master Plan 2025–2030.* [link](https://www.atp.gob.pa/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/PMTS-2025-2030-Final-compilado.pdf)

**What this means for the film**
The evidence is strong for Mexico and Colombia: US government data, Mexican and Colombian official counts and surgeon surveys all point the same way. Dental is the safest lead, since the CDC names it the #1 type. Cosmetic surgery rests only on surgeon surveys. The evidence for Panama is thin: its own government calls the sector early-stage and little known, so a Panama film should sell clinics on *becoming* visible, not on an existing boom. For Costa Rica, do not use the chamber's "13% of arrivals" figure. The official airport survey shows health as the main reason for only **0.3%** of visitors.
