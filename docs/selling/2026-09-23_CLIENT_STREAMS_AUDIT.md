## Four patterns — the hole, in parts

1. **Nobody asked.** Every prospect was chosen by us from a search result. None had shown
   a buying signal (no job post, no request, no deadline).
2. **The pitch was "you have a problem".** The offer was built from our audit score. But
   **51 of 114 audited MANUAL prospects scored 85+ (grade A)** — we offered a fix to sites
   that are already good. See memory rule "audit 85+ is the credential, never
   you-are-invisible".
3. **One touch, then silence.** Most deals got one or two logged messages. Cold replies
   typically come from follow-ups, which mostly did not happen.
4. **The list itself is partly not businesses.** About 17 of 64 ATLAS deals are named after
   a page or article title, not a company: "TOP 10 de Clínicas Veterinarias en Ciudad de
   Panamá", "10 Reasons to Have a Plastic Surgery in Colombia in 2024", "5 Things You Need
   to Know About Dental Tourism in Costa Rica", "Medical Tourism", "Panamá". Search hits
   were saved as companies. One SERP deal is named `https://www.instagram.com`.
   (Some generic titles may still be a real firm's homepage title — unverified.)

And one operational leak: ATLAS keeps staging 7–8 prospects a week into "I act TODAY",
but nothing has been sent since 31 Aug. **22 unsent deals piled up.** A pipeline that
produces tasks nobody executes looks like activity and is not.

## What this means for the romance-promo test

It **is** the same hole if it is: cold, chosen by us, one touch, "you need this".
It is a different experiment only if all four of these hold:

- **Signal:** each target already sells promos to authors today (public price pages). ✅ in the plan.
- **Offer adds money to them, not a flaw to fix:** "a new item you can sell". ✅
- **Show, not diagnose:** the sample trailer goes first, not an audit. ✅
- **Follow-up is scheduled before the first send:** 3 touches over 14 days (day 0, 5, 12). ❌ **add it.**

Plus the stop rule already in the plan: 30 days, at least one yes, otherwise drop it.
Small n (6–8 targets), each written by hand — not a batch.
