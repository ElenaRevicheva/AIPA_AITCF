## "Where is the guarantee I get paid?" — the answer

**The guarantee is the signed contract, not the email address.** A contract binds
a legal person (Elena Revicheva). An email is an identifier inside a system.
`elre993@` is a credential micro1 issued to her; it is not a party to anything.

**The split protects her.** micro1 controls `elre993@` and can revoke it — which
would cost her Slack, Hubstaff and the Google Group, but **not** her Deel
contract, invoice history or balance, because that account is on an email she
owns. The dangerous configuration is the inverse.

🚩 **If micro1 ever asks her to move her Deel account onto an `@expert.micro1.ai`
address, refuse and escalate.** That would hand her counterparty control of her
own payment records.

### Where the real risk actually sits

1. **She does not hold the evidence.** Hours live in Hubstaff inside micro1's org.
   In a dispute the timesheet is in their system. Same shape as the EspaLuz

2. **The link step is undocumented.** If moving hours into Deel is manual and

## Named concept for the interview

**Authentication vs entitlement.** Authentication answers *which account performed
this action*; entitlement answers *who is owed for it*. They live in different
layers, and a mismatch in the first says nothing about the second. Conflating them
means revoking a login accidentally revokes a claim.

> "I keep identity and entitlement in separate layers. Which account performed an
> action and who is owed for it are different questions — if you conflate them,
> revoking someone's login accidentally revokes their claim."
