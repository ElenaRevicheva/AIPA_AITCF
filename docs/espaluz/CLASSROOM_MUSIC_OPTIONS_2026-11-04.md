# EspaLuz classroom — music for ages 6–8 (4 Nov 2026)

Trigger: Alisa liked the music in Buddy.ai. Elena: **not toddler songs** ("Cabeza, hombros, rodillas y pies" is for
toddlers). Alisa is finishing kinder and goes into grado primero: it must fit what international schools do at 6–8,
and carry no copyright risk.

## What Buddy.ai actually shows (checked 4 Nov 2026)
- Official page (buddy.ai/en/english-for-kids): **ages 3–7**; warm-up talk, new words with flashcards, animations,
  video and sounds, voice practice, games, cartoons. **No songs mentioned** there.
- App catalogs (weak sources) say lessons include "songs". Buddy's floor is age 3 — part of why its music can feel young.

## What international schools do with music at 6–8 (primary source)
Cambridge Primary Music, curriculum outline, ages 5–11 (cambridgeinternational.org, 2019):
- Two strands: **Making music** and **Making sense of music**.
- Stage 2 objective examples: "contributing to performances with mostly accurate timing and awareness of **pulse and
  melodic shape**"; "**listening and responding** to music through **asking and answering questions** and making
  sounds and movement".
- Learners explore music "as performers, composers and listeners", with "music from their **own culture** as well as
  … other times and places", using instruments "of any kind (as well as **found objects** and **music technology**)".
US National Core Arts Standards use the same shape (create, perform, respond, connect); grade-level wording not
verified here.

So at 6–8 the job is not sing-along nursery songs. It is **rhythm and pulse, listening with questions, making her own
music, and music from Panama and the world.**

## Formats that fit her age (none of them babyish)
1. **Rhythm echo game ("Ritmo de Titi")** — Titi plays a short clap/drum pattern, she copies it by tapping; patterns
   grow longer. Sounds synthesised in the browser (Web Audio) = **no recording, no copyright**. Cambridge: pulse, timing.
2. **Listening corner ("¿Qué escuchas? / What do you hear?")** — 30–60 s of real classical music whose composition is
   public domain (Saint-Saëns *Carnival of the Animals*: lion, aquarium, elephant; Vivaldi *Spring*; Grieg *In the Hall
   of the Mountain King*). Pere and Titi ask: fast or slow? loud or soft? which animal? how does it make you feel?
   Cambridge: listening and responding by asking and answering. **Recordings must be public-domain ones** (Musopen /
   Wikimedia Commons PD Mark) — check each file's licence before use.
3. **Bilingual rhythm chants / raps** (the Jazz Chants method, Carolyn Graham, NYU — rhythm, stress and intonation of
   real speech) with our own lyrics from each unit: routines, Panama, school. Feels "big kid", teaches real sentences.
4. **Make your own beat** — a small pad of 4–6 sounds (drum, maraca, clap, tambor panameño) she arranges into a loop
   and plays to Pere and Titi. Cambridge: composer + music technology.
5. **Music of Panama** — the tamborito and cumbia rhythms clapped and explained in one line (own culture).
6. Later, optional: original songs in a modern style (acoustic pop, light reggaeton), never nursery melodies —
   ElevenLabs Music can sing our own lyrics; **verify the plan allows commercial use first.**

## Never
Popular children's songs or cartoon songs (Baby Shark, Disney, Cocomelon…) — recordings and lyrics are protected.
Someone else's recording of a public-domain melody is also protected.

## Recommended first step (not built — waiting for Elena's go)

objectives, and both fit inside the existing classroom as one new 🎵 button — nothing existing changes.
