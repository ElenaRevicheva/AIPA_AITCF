**Title:** For families who moved to a Spanish-speaking country: what helps with "life in Spanish", beyond vocabulary?

**Body:**
Our family arrived in Panamá, and the hard part was never the vocabulary list. It was the note from the teacher, the clinic's
front desk, working out what "ahorita" means (anything from "right now" to "maybe never").

Three things help:
1. Asking about the real situation, not the vocabulary. "The teacher wrote X, what do I answer?"
2. Rehearsing the conversation before it happens. A school meeting goes very differently when you've said it out loud once.
3. Ten minutes a day for the kid, every day, rather than an hour on Saturdays.

I built a small tool around exactly that, from my own family's experience (WhatsApp/Telegram/web, remembers the family, a short daily class for
kids 5-8). Happy to share the link if the mods allow it. Mostly curious: what was the thing that actually worked for your family?
