## 2 · Likely questions

**Q: How would you judge if a Russian speaker sounds native?**
"I listen in layers. First, the overall impression: does it sound like a person from Russia?
Then I check specific things. Word stress. Vowel reduction — natives say 'малако', not 'молоко'.
Soft and hard consonants. And intonation — especially questions, where Russian uses the voice, not word order.
Then fluency: pauses, hesitations, and word choice."

**Q: What are typical signs of a non-native speaker or a synthetic voice?**
"Wrong stress is the biggest one — for example 'звОнит' instead of 'звонИт', or 'зАмок' when the text means 'замОк'.
Full vowels where a native reduces them.
Hard consonants where they should be soft.
Flat, robotic intonation, or pauses in the wrong place.
Reading 'всё' as 'все', because the letter ё was written as е.
And unnatural phrases translated from English, like 'это делает смысл'."

**Q: Is a regional accent non-native?**
"No. That is an important line. A speaker from Vologda who says full 'o', or a southern speaker with a soft 'г', is still native.
I would note the regional feature, but not lower the nativeness score for it, unless the guidelines say so.
Native does not mean Moscow standard."

**Q: How do you write your feedback?**
"Short and concrete. I give the timestamp, the word, what I heard, and what a native would say.
For example: 'At 0:07, stress on "звОнит" — native standard is "звонИт". Minor, but a clear non-native marker.'
Then the overall score, and one sentence on why."

**Q: How do you stay consistent across many clips?**
"I follow the guideline first, not my taste.
I keep a few reference clips in mind — a clearly native one and a clearly non-native one — and compare against them.
I use the same words for the same issue every time.
And if I'm unsure, I say so and flag it, instead of guessing."

**Q: Tell me about a time you gave detailed, careful feedback.**
"I built a scoring rubric and an AI judge for my own job search system.
When I replayed fifty-one items it had rejected, twenty were wrong.
I found them because I checked each decision against the rule, one by one.
I also keep a public record of twenty-two written incident reports: what happened, why, and what the pattern is called."

**Q: Do you have audio experience?**
"Yes. I built an automated film pipeline. It places each voice line on its clip,
lowers the music when the voice speaks, and sets the final loudness to broadcast level.
I've published eight films with it. So I'm used to listening closely to recorded speech."

**Q: Do you have a background in linguistics or phonetics?**
"Not a formal degree. My training is practical: I write and publish in Russian,
and I worked for years in legal Russian, where precision matters.
I know the terms — stress, vowel reduction, palatalisation, intonation — and I can explain them clearly in English."

**Q: What would you do if a guideline is unclear?**
"I would not guess. I would write down the exact clip and the question, and ask the project coordinator.
Then I would apply the answer the same way to every similar clip."

**Q: Why do you want this role?**
"It uses my native Russian, my English, and my habit of explaining decisions in writing.
And it helps AI speak Russian more naturally. That matters to me: Russian is my first language."

## 4 · Words to explain in English (not read aloud)

| Term | Plain meaning | Russian example |
|---|---|---|
| Word stress | Which syllable is strong | звонИт, не звОнит |
| Vowel reduction | Unstressed vowels get weaker | молоко → [малако] |
| Palatalisation | "Soft" consonants | мат vs мать |
| Final devoicing | A voiced consonant at the end sounds voiceless | хлеб → [хлеп] |
| Intonation contour | How the pitch rises and falls | question vs statement |
| Prosody / rhythm | The music of the sentence | pauses, speed, flow |
| Register | Formal or informal style | вы vs ты, bookish vs spoken |
| Calque | Word-for-word copy from another language | "это делает смысл" |
| Disfluency | Hesitations, restarts, fillers | "э-э", "ну", repeats |
| TTS | Text-to-speech, a synthetic voice | robotic, flat pitch |
| Homograph | Same spelling, different stress and meaning | зАмок / замОк |
